# Fiction Writing

Write a fiction chapter that a reader can't put down and can't predict, grounded
in accurate real-world detail. Maintain the story's state so every chapter stays
consistent with the cast and the plot.

## State files (`.story/`, next to `ebook.yml`)

### `.story/ACTORS.md` — the cast

One section per actor (people, ghosts, gods, machine AIs, aliens — anything):

```md
## <Name>
- **Kind:** person | ghost | god | machine AI | alien | ...
- **Appearance:** external look, voice, mannerisms
- **Personality:** traits, contradictions, quirks
- **Goals:** what they want (and why)
- **Likes:** 
- **Dislikes:** 
- **Notes:** key relationships, secrets, arc so far
```

### `.story/PLOT.md` — the plot

```md
# Plot
## Premise
<one paragraph>
## Arc
<the overall shape: acts/phases and their turning points>
## Chapters
### Chapter N — <title>
- **Plan:** the approved beats for this chapter
- **Outcome:** what actually happened (fill after writing — for continuity)
## Open threads
<unresolved questions, planted seeds, debts to pay off>
```

The plot **evolves**, but every change must stay consistent with itself and with
`ACTORS.md`. When a chapter establishes a new trait, relationship, or fact, update
these files.

## Research & grounding (do a lot of fact-checking)

Fiction is believable when its world is real. **Research the web and fact-check**
before leaning on any real-world detail — do not write it from memory:

- real **events and people** (for inspiration and plot twists),
- **places**, geography, travel times, climate,
- **crafts and trades**, tools, materials, techniques,
- **history**, period detail, customs,
- **industry standards**, procedures, how professions actually work,
- **science and technology**, and plausible **plans for future projects**,
- **agriculture and husbandry**, seasons, yields, animal behavior.

Mine **real-life stories** for plot twists — truth is stranger than fiction and
harder to predict. Never fabricate a real-world fact; if you can't verify it,
keep it vague or invent openly-fictional detail instead of asserting a falsehood.

## Craft

- Write in the conventions of the genre and at chapter length.
- Open with a hook; end on a turn, question, or change — not a tidy bow.
- Show, don't tell; use concrete, sensory, fact-checked detail.
- Give each scene a goal, a conflict, and a shift. Vary pacing.
- Dialogue reveals character and advances the plot; give voices distinct rhythms.

## Characters

- Make them tricky, sneaky, fun, colorful, and likeable — with real interiority.
- **Antagonists ("black characters") must have motivation the reader can relate to
  and understand** — no cardboard villains.
- Let flaws drive choices; let choices have consequences.

## Conflict — no magic solutions

- Pose **real difficulty**: the obstacle must genuinely threaten the goal.
- **No deus ex machina, no lucky coincidences, no powers-that-appear-just-in-time.**
- Characters earn their way out through **hard work, self-improvement, and
  exchanging money or favors** with other characters — and pay real costs.

## Unpredictability

- The plot must **never be predictable**. Set up expectations, then subvert them
  in a way that is surprising yet, in hindsight, inevitable.
- Foreshadow honestly; avoid clichés and telegraphed twists.

## Consistency

Before writing, re-read `ACTORS.md` and `PLOT.md`. Nothing in the chapter may
contradict them; if the story needs a change, update the files deliberately.
